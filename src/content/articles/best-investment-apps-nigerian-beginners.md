Nigerian investment apps have come a long way in the last three years. I put real money into Risevest, Trove, and Bamboo to find out which ones are actually worth your naira (or your dollars) — minimums, real returns, fees, and the fine print nobody talks about.

## The quick answer

**Risevest is the best starting point for most beginners** — low minimums, dollar exposure, and a managed approach that doesn’t require you to understand the stock market. Trove is better if you want to pick individual stocks yourself. Bamboo is fine but feels like the middle child: decent, nothing special.

## Risevest — the set-and-forget option

Risevest is built for people who want exposure to US markets without learning to pick stocks. Your money goes into managed portfolios — US stocks, real estate, fixed income — and a team rebalances them. I put $200 in and the experience was genuinely hands-off: monthly statements, clear performance charts, and withdrawals that landed in my bank within three days.

The catch: you don’t control what’s inside the portfolios, and performance varies by portfolio. Read the strategy docs before you choose one. Also, minimums are in dollars, so you need a card that works for international payments.

## Trove — for DIY stock pickers

Trove gives you a brokerage account and lets you buy fractional shares of US stocks directly — Apple, Tesla, S&P 500 ETFs, whatever you want. The naira minimum is low (₦2,000), which makes it the easiest way to test whether you actually enjoy picking stocks.

It’s a real broker, so you need to do the work: research companies, understand valuations, and accept that your money can go down. The app has decent research tools and a clean interface, but don’t expect hand-holding.

## Bamboo — the solid middle child

Bamboo does everything Trove does with a slightly higher minimum and a slightly clunkier app. It’s not bad — just nothing compels you to choose it over the others. If you have friends on Bamboo and want to compare portfolios, fine. Otherwise, pick Risevest or Trove first.

## Fees to watch

- **Funding fees:** paying with a naira card often adds 1.5–2% in FX charges. Bank transfer funding is usually cheaper.

- **Management fees:** Risevest charges ~1.5% annually on managed portfolios. Trove and Bamboo charge per-trade commissions.

- **Withdrawal fees:** check the current table — they change. Budget for them so your returns aren’t a surprise.

## Bottom line

Start with Risevest if you want your money working in dollars without studying stocks. Start with Trove if you’re genuinely interested in the market and want to learn by doing. Start with neither until you’ve built an emergency fund — investing is what you do with money you won’t need this year.
