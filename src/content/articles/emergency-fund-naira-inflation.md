Six months of expenses, says the textbook. The textbook was written for 2% inflation. When prices rise 20%+ a year, a static emergency fund melts — ₦1.2 million today buys what ₦1 million bought twelve months ago. The rule needs a Nigerian adjustment.

## The inflation-adjusted formula

Target = monthly essential expenses × 6 × 1.2. The extra 20% is one year of high inflation pre-loaded, so the fund still covers six real months a year from now. Spend ₦200,000 monthly on essentials? Your target is ₦1.44 million, not ₦1.2 million. Revisit the number every January — stale targets are the whole problem.

## Essentials means essentials

Rent share, food, transport, data, power, minimum debt payments. Not Netflix, not brunch. The most common failure is sizing the fund on total spending, hitting an impossible target, and abandoning the project. A lean fund you actually build beats a complete fund you never start.

## Where to keep it

Instant access, zero volatility, interest as a bonus: a flexible high-yield savings plan or money-market fund with same-day withdrawal. Not stocks, not crypto, not a fixed lock you pay penalties to break — emergencies do not schedule around tenor dates. Split across two apps if the total exceeds your comfort with any single platform.

## Build it in three tiers

Tier one: ₦100,000 fast, in 30 days — sells something, pause one subscription, done. Tier two: one month of essentials, automated monthly. Tier three: the full inflation-adjusted six months.
