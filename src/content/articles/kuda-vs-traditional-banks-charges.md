"Free transfers" is the headline. The real comparison is everything around it: card issuance, maintenance fees, reversal speed when transfers fail, and whether support answers when money is stuck. I ran both side by side for six months. Here is the honest maths.

## The fee stack, in naira per year

Assume 20 transfers a month, one debit card, and average balances:

| Cost line | Typical old-gen bank | Kuda |
|---|---|---|
| Transfer fees (240/yr) | ~₦12,000–₦24,000 | ₦0 (fair-use cap applies) |
| Account maintenance | ~₦4,000–₦12,000/yr | ₦0 |
| Debit card issuance | ~₦1,000–₦1,500 | ~₦1,000–₦1,500 |
| SMS alerts | ~₦4–₦6 per alert | Free in-app notifications |
| **Honest total** | **~₦17,000–₦38,000/yr** | **~₦1,000–₦1,500/yr** |

The gap is real but not life-changing — roughly one bag of rice. The bigger win is behavioural: free transfers remove the "let me batch payments" friction that leaves bills unpaid.

## Where traditional banks still win

Failed-transfer reversals at big banks typically resolve in 24–48 hours through established dispute desks; digital-bank reversals can take longer when the receiving bank drags. Cash deposits are the other gap — agency-banking workarounds exist but a branch network still matters if you handle cash weekly. And for loans above ₦1 million, traditional banks' structured products remain cheaper than digital overdrafts.

## Support, tested

Response time on in-app chat averaged under 10 minutes in my tests; phone support at traditional banks averaged 20+ minutes of hold music. But when a transfer above ₦500,000 got stuck, the bank with a branch resolved it same-day with a stamped printout — paper still talks in Nigeria.

## The setup that actually works

Run both. Salary lands in the traditional account (employer quirks, dollar options, branch access), a standing instruction sweeps spending money to Kuda weekly, and bills go out free from there. Two apps, ten minutes of setup, ₦20,000+ saved yearly.
