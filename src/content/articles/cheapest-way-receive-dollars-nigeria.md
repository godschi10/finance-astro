Every dollar you receive passes through three tolls: the transfer fee, the exchange-rate margin, and the withdrawal or conversion charge. Most comparisons quote only the first. Here are all three, for the corridors Nigerians actually use.

## The three-amount test

For a $200 freelance payout, a $1,000 salary transfer, and a $5,000 family support payment:

- **$200:** fintech rails (Grey, Geegpay) win. Flat fees under $2 and near-mid-market conversion beat Wise's percentage fee at this size, and domiciliary accounts lose badly to maintenance plus conversion spreads.
- **$1,000:** Wise edges ahead on pure rate, Grey ties when you keep dollars unconverted — which you should if you spend in dollars anyway.
- **$5,000:** domiciliary accounts finally compete, because flat wire fees spread thin. But only if your bank's conversion spread stays under 2% — confirm before routing, spreads move.

## The margin nobody quotes

A "0 fee" transfer at 3% below mid-market costs $30 per $1,000 — triple Wise's headline fee. Always compare by **what lands in naira**, never by the fee line. Our money-transfer comparator tool does exactly this maths with current numbers.

## Speed vs cost

Instant payout costs 1–2% more than 1–2 day settlement on most corridors. For rent-day emergencies, pay it. For recurring salary, take the slow rail and pocket the difference — over a year of $1,000 monthly transfers, that is $120–$240 back.

## Rules for every transfer

Get quotes from two providers every time — loyalty to one app costs money. Keep dollars as dollars until you need naira; converting early donates the spread. And never let anyone "help" you receive funds through their account: the fraud stories all start there.
