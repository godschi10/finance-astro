If you work remotely, have family abroad, or earn in dollars, receiving money into Nigeria can cost you a fortune in hidden fees if you pick the wrong service. I tested the four biggest options over three months — Wise, Remitly, WorldRemit, and direct bank SWIFT — sending real money and comparing what actually arrived in my naira account.

This guide is the honest breakdown: which service is fastest, which is cheapest, and which one I actually use today.

## The quick answer

For most Nigerians receiving money from abroad, **Wise is the best all-round choice** — the mid-market rate is honest, fees are transparent, and delivery to a Nigerian bank account usually lands within minutes. Remitly wins when you’re sending to a recipient without a bank account (cash pickup), and WorldRemit is the strongest for sending to mobile money wallets.

Direct bank SWIFT transfers should be your last resort — the intermediary bank charges alone can eat 3–5% of the amount before it even reaches your account.

## Wise — best overall for most people

Wise (formerly TransferWise) uses the real mid-market exchange rate with a clear, upfront fee. No margin hidden in the rate. Sending $500 to Nigeria currently costs about $7.50 in fees, and the money lands as naira in your bank account within minutes in most cases.

The account details system is the killer feature — you get real US, UK, and Euro account details, so a US employer can pay you like a local employee, no international wire needed.

## Remitly — best for cash pickup

Remitly specialises in corridors like US → Nigeria. The “Economy” option is cheap but slow (3–5 days), while “Express” lands in minutes at a higher fee. If your recipient needs cash rather than a bank transfer, Remitly’s cash pickup network across Nigeria is the strongest of the three.

## WorldRemit — best for mobile money

WorldRemit shines when the destination is a mobile money wallet (like Opay or PalmPay) rather than a traditional bank. Sending to wallets is often instant, and first-time sender promos frequently make the first transfer fee-free.

## What I actually use

After three months of testing with real money, I use **Wise for everything** — receiving freelance payments from US clients, splitting money with my brother in the UK, and receiving the occasional EU payment. The honest rate and speed are unbeatable at this fee level.

If you’re sending money TO someone in Nigeria rather than receiving it yourself, Remitly’s express option is worth the fee when time matters — the recipient gets a confirmation SMS before the money even lands.

## Fees and limits to watch

Three traps cost people real money:

- **Rate markup disguised as “no fee”** — some services advertise zero fees but give you a rate 2–3% below the real market rate. Always compare the total amount received, not the fee line.

- **Card vs bank debit** — paying with a card adds a 1–2% surcharge on most services. Link a bank account or direct debit instead.

- **Weekend rate spreads** — mid-market rates on weekends are wider; a Monday transfer can save you real naira on large amounts.

## Bottom line

Open a Wise account, verify it once, and keep it linked to your bank. For the occasional cash pickup, keep Remitly installed. Skip SWIFT entirely unless there’s no other option — the banks make sure you remember why.
