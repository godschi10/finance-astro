You do not need a six-figure salary to own Nigerian stocks. ₦50,000 a month, invested mechanically through the NGX, beats waiting years to "have enough to start." The method is dollar-cost averaging: same amount, same schedule, whatever the market is doing.

## Why averaging beats timing

Nobody rings a bell at market bottoms. When you buy a fixed naira amount monthly, you automatically buy more shares when prices are low and fewer when they are high. Over two to three years, your average entry price smooths out the drama — and the drama on the NGX is real.

## Pick a broker first

Any SEC-licensed broker with an app and low minimums works. Compare three things: account opening fully in-app with BVN, visible fee schedule (brokerage around 1.35% plus CSCS and NSE fees totalling roughly 2% per trade), and automatic dividend reinvestment or at least free dividend mandates. Open one account and stop shopping — broker-hopping is procrastination in disguise.

## The ₦50,000 monthly split

A worked example, not advice — adjust the names to your own research:

- **₦25,000** into a broad NGX index or ETF position. Boring, and that is the point.
- **₦15,000** into one dividend-paying blue chip you understand well enough to explain in two sentences.
- **₦10,000** held as dry powder in a money-market fund for months when quality names dip 10% or more.

At roughly 2% all-in fees, each ₦50,000 deployment costs about ₦1,000 in friction. Monthly is the sweet spot — weekly investing would let fees eat you alive.

## Rules that keep you in the game

Automate the transfer for the day after payday. Never sell because of a headline; sell only because the business thesis broke. Review once a quarter, not once an hour — checking prices daily converts investors into gamblers.

## What this compounds to

₦50,000 monthly at a 12% average annual return is roughly ₦4.1 million after five years, of which ₦3 million is your contributions. The extra ₦1.1 million is the market paying you for patience. Start this month; the best entry point was five years ago and the second-best is payday.
